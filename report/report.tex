\begin{abstract}
Environmental sound recognition (ESR) is essential for autonomous acoustic monitoring, yet out-of-distribution acoustic corruptions severely degrade real-world performance. In this work, we present a systematic multi-label benchmarking study on FSD50K under both clean and heavily corrupted acoustic conditions. We evaluate Convolutional Neural Network baselines (ResNet-18/34, EfficientNet-B0/B4) against Audio Spectrogram Transformers (ViT-Tiny/Small). While convolutional architectures achieve strong clean recognition (EfficientNet-B4 reaching $0.5396$ mAP and EfficientNet-B0 providing exceptional parameter efficiency with $4.26\text{M}$ parameters), Vision Transformers provide competitive discrimination and Top-1 retrieval ($76.54\%$ for ViT-Small). Under heavy $-5\text{ dB}$ SNR room noise corruption from the out-of-distribution ACE benchmark, undefended ViT-Tiny performance degrades from $0.5068$ to $0.2040$ mAP. To address this degradation, we develop an acoustic augmentation pipeline combining continuous spectral-slope colored noise ($1/f^\alpha$, $\alpha \in [0, 2]$), Schroeder room reverberation, Mixup ($\alpha=0.5$), and learned attention pooling. The resulting robust model improves $-5\text{ dB}$ performance to $0.3182$ mAP ($+56.0\%$ relative gain, $+0.1142$ absolute mAP over baseline ViT-Tiny) while reaching $0.5303$ clean mAP without external noise datasets. Finally, extensive ablations quantify the trade-offs of token pooling heads, decision threshold calibration, and individual augmentation components.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} Environmental Sound Recognition, Audio Spectrogram Transformer, Noise Robustness, Digital Signal Processing Augmentation, FSD50K, Multi-Label Classification.

\section{Introduction}
Environmental Sound Classification (ESC) and multi-label Sound Event Recognition (SER) aim to identify and categorize sound events occurring in unstructured acoustic environments. Unlike human speech or Western musical compositions, environmental sounds are highly non-stationary, exhibit diverse spectral-temporal morphology (ranging from sharp acoustic transients to broadband ambient hums), and frequently overlap in polyphonic mixtures.

\subsection{The Real-World Acoustic Domain Gap}
While deep learning models have achieved impressive recognition benchmarks on curated, clean audio datasets such as FSD50K~\cite{fonseca2021fsd50k} and AudioSet~\cite{gemmeke2017audioset}, real-world acoustic deployments present a severe domain shift. In applications such as Internet of Things (IoT) acoustic monitoring, hearing prosthetics, and autonomous navigation, incoming acoustic signals are continuously contaminated by ambient background noise (traffic, HVAC systems, wind, crowds). Most existing academic benchmarks evaluate models exclusively on identically distributed clean splits, obscuring the catastrophic failure modes that occur under adverse Signal-to-Noise Ratios (SNR).

\subsection{Research Questions}
To systematically bridge this gap, this investigation is structured around four concrete Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Architectural Inductive Bias):} How do modern Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures compare against standard Convolutional Neural Networks (ResNet, EfficientNet) in multi-label environmental sound classification regarding recognition accuracy (mAP as primary metric, Top-1 hit and mAUC secondary), parameter efficiency, and computational cost?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Token Pooling Representations):} Given that Vision Transformers produce hundreds of spatial patch tokens over an audio spectrogram, how do different pooling mechanisms (Dual-Token $\text{CLS}+\text{DIST}$ pooling vs. Global Average Pooling vs. GAP+Max vs. Learned Attention Pooling) affect multi-label sound discrimination?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Acoustic Noise Robustness):} How severely does model performance degrade under varying signal-to-noise ratios (SNR from $+10\text{ dB}$ down to $-5\text{ dB}$), and can purely synthetic DSP augmentations (continuous colored noise, Schroeder reverberation) induce acoustic invariance without relying on external noise data?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Augmentation Component Contributions):} What are the individual and synergistic contributions of time-masking, frequency-masking, and Mixup in regularizing multi-label spectrogram classification, and how does decision threshold calibration resolve tail-class recall compression?
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{CNNs vs. Transformers in Audio Classification}
Historically, deep audio recognition relied on 2D Convolutional Neural Networks applied to time-frequency representations (Mel-spectrograms). Architectures such as PANNs (CNN-14)~\cite{kong2020panns} and ResNet~\cite{he2016deep} treat spectrograms as single-channel images, leveraging local receptive fields and translation equivariance.

Recently, the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST)~\cite{gong2021ast} adapted Vision Transformers (ViT) and Data-efficient Image Transformers (DeiT)~\cite{touvron2021training} to audio. By dividing spectrograms into overlapping 2D patches and employing multi-head self-attention, AST captures non-local temporal-spectral dependencies across distant frequency bands (such as fundamental pitches and harmonic overtones).

\subsection{Data Augmentation in Audio}
Spectrogram data augmentation is essential for regularizing high-capacity neural networks. SpecAugment~\cite{park2019specaugment} applies random blocks of frequency masking and time masking, preventing models from over-fitting to localized spectral artifacts. Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} performs convex combinations of audio pairs and their corresponding multi-hot ground truth labels:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \lambda \mathbf{x}_i + (1 - \lambda) \mathbf{x}_j, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{y}} = \lambda \mathbf{y}_i + (1 - \lambda) \mathbf{y}_j
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \alpha)$. In multi-label sound event recognition, Mixup naturally reflects the physical principle of acoustic wave superposition.

\subsection{Robustness and Acoustic Corruption Benchmarks}
Although speech recognition has standardized noise benchmarks (such as NOISEX-92 and the ACE Corpus~\cite{eaton2016ace}), open-domain environmental sound recognition lacks standardized out-of-distribution noise evaluation pipelines. Room reverberation modeling via statistical impulse responses~\cite{schroeder1962natural} offers a principled, physics-informed mechanism to simulate reverberant propagation. Prior works often report accuracy exclusively on clean test splits, failing to quantify model robustness when tested under real acoustic degradation.

\section{Dataset and Acoustic Environment Benchmark}

\subsection{Primary Dataset: FSD50K}
We conduct all primary model training and validation on FSD50K~\cite{fonseca2021fsd50k} (Version 1.0), an open dataset of human-labeled sound events drawn from the AudioSet Ontology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Official URL:} \url{https://zenodo.org/record/4060432}
    \item \textbf{Vocabulary:} 200 sound classes hierarchically organized into musical instruments, human sounds, domestic sounds, and environmental soundscapes.
    \item \textbf{Split Partitions:} Standard evaluation split consisting of a Development set (36,796 training clips, $\sim 69$ hours; 4,170 validation clips, $\sim 11$ hours; totaling $\sim 80$ hours) and a held-out Evaluation split (10,231 clips, $\sim 28$ hours).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Acoustic Noise Benchmark: ACE Dataset}
To establish a rigorous out-of-distribution evaluation benchmark without contaminating training splits, we utilize the Acoustic Characterization of Environments (ACE) corpus~\cite{eaton2016ace} (\url{https://zenodo.org/records/6257551?}). The ACE corpus provides high-fidelity ambient background noises recorded across diverse indoor acoustic spaces (offices, lecture halls, meeting rooms) containing genuine noise sources such as HVAC ventilation fans, ambient human speech babble, and computer cooling equipment. We construct a standardized out-of-distribution evaluation suite by mixing the 10,231 clean held-out FSD50K evaluation clips with calibrated ACE ambient noises across four standardized Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) tiers: $+10\text{ dB}$ (mild interference), $+5\text{ dB}$ (moderate interference), $0\text{ dB}$ (equal power), and $-5\text{ dB}$ (heavy noise corruption), resulting in $40,924$ corrupted evaluation instances.

\subsection{Audio Preprocessing Pipeline}
All audio signals are standardized to $16,000\text{ Hz}$ mono PCM audio. Clips are framed into fixed $10.0\text{ s}$ windows ($160,000$ samples), using random cropping during training and center cropping during validation/testing. Clips shorter than $10.0\text{ s}$ are zero-padded.

Log-Mel spectrograms are extracted using Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Window length: $25\text{ ms}$ ($400$ samples), Hop length: $10\text{ ms}$ ($160$ samples).
    \item Mel filterbank: $N_{\text{mels}} = 128$ bins covering $0\text{ Hz}$ to $8,000\text{ Hz}$.
    \item Logarithmic scaling: $S(t, f) = \log(\text{Mel}(t, f) + 10^{-6})$.
    \item Global standardization: $\tilde{S} = \frac{S - \mu}{2\sigma}$ using standard AudioSet pre-training statistics ($\mu = -4.2677$, $\sigma = 4.5690$) adopted from AST~\cite{gong2021ast} to maintain distribution alignment with pre-trained visual/audio weights. Target input tensor shape is $1 \times 128 \times 1000$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Convolutional Baseline Architectures vs. Audio Spectrogram Transformers}
\label{sec:cnn_baselines}
Audio spectrograms represent acoustic energy across continuous time (horizontal axis) and frequency (vertical axis). Treating a log-Mel spectrogram as an optical image introduces fundamental physical discrepancies that differentiate Convolutional Neural Networks from Vision Transformers.

In optical computer vision, 2D convolution leverages spatial translation equivariance: an object retains its identity regardless of spatial coordinates. In audio spectrograms, the temporal axis is translation-invariant (a sound event occurring at $t=2\text{ s}$ vs. $t=6\text{ s}$ retains its category), but the frequency axis is \textbf{strictly non-shift-invariant}. Shifting spectral energy upward alters pitch, formant structure, and harmonic intervals, fundamentally changing sound identity. Standard CNNs (ResNet-18/34, EfficientNet-B0/B4) apply small convolutional kernels that excel at localized spectro-temporal textures and onset bursts, but require deep layer hierarchies and spatial pooling to capture multi-octave harmonic coherence (e.g., fundamental pitches at $100\text{ Hz}$ and integer overtones at $1\text{--}4\text{ kHz}$).

In contrast, the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) divides the 2D spectrogram into patches and applies multi-head self-attention. This establishes an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ information path length between any pair of time-frequency patches, allowing early self-attention layers to directly attend to simultaneous harmonic overtones across the entire 128 Mel channels. Learnable positional embeddings encode both temporal offset and frequency band index, capturing non-shift-invariant spectral structures naturally. To transfer visual pre-training without RGB channel expansion, the initial convolutional layer (\texttt{conv1}) of ResNet and EfficientNet is adapted from 3 channels to 1 single spectrogram channel by summing pre-trained weights across the channel dimension ($\mathbf{W}_{\text{conv1\_new}} = \sum_{c=1}^3 \mathbf{W}_{\text{orig}}[:, c, :, :]$), consistent with our energy preservation implementation in the AST patch projection layer.


\subsection{Main Proposed Method: Audio Spectrogram Transformer}
Our primary classification architecture is the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) built upon DeiT ViT-Tiny and ViT-Small backbones:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Patch Tokenization:} The 2D spectrogram $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 1000}$ is divided into $P \times P$ patches ($16 \times 16$) with a stride of 10 pixels (an overlap of $6$ pixels in both time and frequency), producing $N = 1,188$ patch tokens.
    \item \textbf{Linear Projection and Positional Embeddings:} Each patch is flattened and linearly projected into dimension $D$ ($D=192$ for ViT-Tiny, $D=384$ for ViT-Small). Whereas original AST~\cite{gong2021ast} averages weights across the 3 RGB channels, our implementation adapts the pre-trained DeiT 3-channel patch projection weights to single-channel spectrograms by summing across the channel dimension ($\mathbf{W}_{\text{patch\_1ch}} = \sum_{c=1}^3 \mathbf{W}_{\text{patch\_3ch}}[:, c, :, :]$). Unlike convolutional layers followed by immediate normalization, ViT adds the linear projection directly to $\mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}$; channel summation preserves total activation energy between projected patch content and pre-trained positional embeddings:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_0 = [\mathbf{x}_{\text{CLS}}; \mathbf{x}_{\text{DIST}}; \mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}_p^1; \dots; \mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}_p^N] + \mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{x}_{\text{CLS}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{DIST}}$ are learnable classification and distillation tokens, and $\mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+2) \times D}$ represents 1D learnable position embeddings initialized from DeiT and interpolated to $1,190$ tokens.
    \item \textbf{Self-Attention Encoder:} The sequence is processed through 12 standard Transformer encoder blocks containing Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and MLP sub-layers with LayerNorm and residual connections.
    \item \textbf{Classification Head:} The output representation is pooled and projected onto the 200 target classes:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}} \cdot \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}) + \mathbf{b}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Token Pooling Mechanisms}
We explore four distinct patch aggregation strategies over the output tokens $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{1188 \times D}$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dual-Token Pooling (Baseline):} Average of the classification and distillation tokens: $\mathbf{h} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{CLS}} + \mathbf{z}_{\text{DIST}})$.
    \item \textbf{Global Average Pooling (GAP):} Uniform mean over all $1,188$ patch tokens: $\mathbf{h} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{z}_i$.
    \item \textbf{GAP + Max Pooling:} Concatenation of mean and peak patch representations: $\mathbf{h} = [\text{mean}(\mathbf{Z}); \text{max}(\mathbf{Z})]$ (dimension $2D$).
    \item \textbf{Attention Pooling:} Softmax-weighted aggregation with learned query vector $\mathbf{w}_a \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha_i = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_a^T \mathbf{z}_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp(\mathbf{w}_a^T \mathbf{z}_j)}, \quad \mathbf{h} = \sum_{i=1}^N \alpha_i \mathbf{z}_i
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Augmentation Techniques}
\label{sec:data_augmentations}
To regularize training and simulate complex acoustic scenes, we evaluate four standard data augmentation methods:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Time Masking:} Zeroes out contiguous temporal frames across all frequency bands ($\Delta t \sim \mathcal{U}(0, T_{\text{mask}})$, up to $T_{\text{mask}} = 192$ frames / $1.92\text{ s}$).
    \item \textbf{Frequency Masking:} Zeroes out contiguous frequency channels across all time steps ($\Delta f \sim \mathcal{U}(0, F_{\text{mask}})$, up to $F_{\text{mask}} = 48$ mel bins).
    \item \textbf{SpecAugment:} The joint application of both Time Masking and Frequency Masking directly on the log-mel spectrogram.
    \item \textbf{Mixup:} Convex linear interpolation of raw audio waveforms and multi-label targets: $\tilde{x} = \lambda x_a + (1 - \lambda) x_b$ and $\tilde{y} = \lambda y_a + (1 - \lambda) y_b$, where $\lambda \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha_{\text{mix}}, \alpha_{\text{mix}})$ with $\alpha_{\text{mix}} = 0.5$. In audio, Mixup physically reflects acoustic wave superposition.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed DSP-Augmented Robust Training Framework}
\label{sec:dsp_augmentations}
Real-world acoustic environments present severe out-of-distribution background noise and reverberation that cause undefended models to collapse. Rather than relying on external, static noise datasets that introduce dataset bias and memory overhead, we propose a purely algorithmic Digital Signal Processing (DSP) augmentation framework that synthesizes realistic acoustic corruptions directly on-the-fly.

\subsubsection{Continuous Spectral-Slope Colored Noise \texorpdfstring{($1/f^\alpha$)}{(1/f\textasciicircum alpha)}}
Physical environmental noise (wind, traffic, ventilation fans) rarely exhibits a flat white-noise spectrum. Natural acoustic ambient spectra follow power-law decays $S(f) \propto 1/f^\alpha$, where $\alpha=0$ represents white noise, $\alpha=1$ represents pink noise, and $\alpha=2$ represents brown/Brownian noise. During training, we synthesize continuous spectral-slope colored noise dynamically on-the-fly:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parameter Sampling:} For each audio clip, we sample the continuous spectral slope parameter $\alpha \sim \mathcal{U}(0.0, 2.0)$ and target Signal-to-Noise Ratio $\gamma_{\text{dB}} \sim \mathcal{U}(-5.0, 20.0)\text{ dB}$ with probability $p=0.5$.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Filtering:} Standard white Gaussian noise $w(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ of length $L = 160,000$ samples ($10.0\text{ s}$ at $16\text{ kHz}$) is transformed to the frequency domain $W(f) = \mathcal{F}\{w(t)\}$. We apply a continuous frequency-domain transfer function $H(f)$:
    \begin{equation}
        H(f) = \frac{1}{\max(f, f_{\text{min}})^{\alpha/2}}, \quad f_{\text{min}} = 10\text{ Hz}
    \end{equation}
    where $f_{\text{min}} = 10\text{ Hz}$ avoids singular division at DC ($f=0$).
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Additive Mixing:} The colored noise is transformed back to the time domain via inverse FFT $n_{\text{raw}}(t) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\{W(f) \cdot H(f)\}$, normalized to unit Root-Mean-Square (RMS) energy, and scaled to match the target SNR $\gamma_{\text{dB}}$ relative to clean audio $x(t)$:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{RMS}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^L x(t)^2}, \quad \sigma_{\text{noise}} = \frac{\text{RMS}(x)}{10^{\gamma_{\text{dB}}/20}}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
        x_{\text{aug}}(t) = x(t) + \sigma_{\text{noise}} \cdot \frac{n_{\text{raw}}(t)}{\text{RMS}(n_{\text{raw}}(t))}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Schroeder Room Reverberation Modeling}
Physical acoustic propagation in enclosed spaces generates early reflections and dense decaying reverberant tails. To simulate realistic acoustic environments without pre-recorded Room Impulse Responses (RIRs), we implement Schroeder's statistical reverberator model~\cite{schroeder1962natural}:
\begin{equation}
    h(t) = e^{-\frac{6.9078 \cdot t}{T_{60}}} \cdot v(t), \quad 0 \le t \le T_{\text{max}}, \quad v(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)
\end{equation}
where $T_{60} \sim \mathcal{U}(0.15, 0.60)\text{ s}$ denotes the reverberation decay time (time required for energy to decay by $60\text{ dB}$), $T_{\text{max}} = 1.0\text{ s}$ ($16,000$ samples), and $v(t)$ is Gaussian white noise modeling diffuse reflections. With probability $p=0.3$, the impulse response is convolved via fast FFT circular convolution $x_{\text{reverb}}(t) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\} \cdot \mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}\}$ and normalized to match the RMS energy of $x(t)$.

\subsubsection{Proposed End-to-End System Specifications}
Our final robust architecture integrates these components into a unified system (Figure~\ref{fig:final_model_architecture}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone Encoder:} ViT-Tiny backbone ($16 \times 16$ patches, stride 10, $N=1,188$ tokens, $D=192$, 12 layers, $5.65\text{M}$ parameters, $13.04\text{ GMACs}$).
    \item \textbf{Classification Head:} Learned Attention Pooling ($\mathbf{w}_a \in \mathbb{R}^{192}$) with softmax weighting over all 1,188 patch tokens, projecting linearly to 200 classes.
    \item \textbf{Streamlined Augmentation Recipe:} Pairwise Mixup ($\text{Beta}(0.5, 0.5)$, $p=0.5$), continuous colored noise ($\alpha \in [0, 2]$, $p=0.5$), Schroeder reverberation ($T_{60} \in [0.15, 0.60]\text{ s}$, $p=0.3$), baseline additive Gaussian noise ($\text{NL}=0.05$), time shift ($\pm 10$ frames), and class-balanced sampling (\texttt{Distributed}\-\texttt{Weighted}\-\texttt{Sampler}). SpecAugment is disabled ($\text{FM}=0, \text{TM}=0$) to prevent destructive over-masking when combined with waveform DSP noise.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparison Strategy and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:comparison_strategy}
To ensure fair and rigorous benchmarking across distinct architectural families, we establish standardized evaluation protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standardized Splits:} All models are evaluated on the official, held-out FSD50K evaluation split ($10,231$ clips, $\sim 28.0$ hours). Out-of-distribution robustness is benchmarked on the $40,924$ synthesized ACE noise evaluation instances across four SNR tiers (+10~dB, +5~dB, 0~dB, -5~dB).
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Metrics:} Our primary benchmark metric is Mean Average Precision (mAP), computing the macro-averaged Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) across all 200 classes:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{mAP} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C \sum_{k=1}^K P_c(k) \Delta R_c(k)
    \end{equation}
    Because multi-label environmental audio is sparse ($>99.3\%$ negative class priors), PR-AUC prioritizes true positive discrimination while penalizing false positives. Secondary metrics include Top-1 Hit Accuracy, calibrated Micro-F1 / Macro-F1 at operational deployment threshold $\tau = 0.20$, and Mean Area Under ROC Curve (mAUC).
    \item \textbf{Optimization Regimes:} Both CNN and Transformer architectures are trained for 30 epochs with AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), multi-label BCEWithLogitsLoss, label smoothing ($0.1$), and the \texttt{ast\_step} decay schedule (5 epochs constant initial LR, then $0.90\times$ decay per epoch). Following standard practice in AST~\cite{gong2021ast} and CMKD~\cite{gong2022cmkd}, base learning rates are set to $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for CNNs and $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for Transformers. Learning rates were maintained at these established literature defaults without exhaustive per-family grid sweeps, which due to practical constraint.
\end{enumerate}

For single-seed evaluations, we adopt a practical effect-size threshold of $\Delta \ge 0.010$ ($1.0$ point mAP) as our experimental rule of thumb; by this benchmark, the $0.0074$ mAP margin between Mixup and Full at $-5\text{ dB}$ falls within the practical indifference zone. \subsubsection{Ablation Study 2: Decision Threshold Sweeping and Probability Calibration}
\label{sec:threshold_sweeping}
In standard multi-label pipelines, a default decision threshold of $\tau = 0.50$ is conventionally assumed. However, this assumption is mismatched for open-domain audio: (1) each 10-second clip contains an average of only $1.3$ ground-truth labels out of 200 classes ($>99.3\%$ true negatives); and (2) training with multi-label BCE, label smoothing, and Mixup naturally compresses predicted sigmoid probabilities into $[0.15, 0.40]$. Evaluating at $\tau = 0.50$ incurs a severe recall penalty ($52.0\%\text{--}55.1\%$).

Systematic sweeping across $\tau \in [0.10, 0.70]$ on validation clips (full trajectory in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:threshold_sweep}) reveals that lowering $\tau$ from $0.50$ to $0.30$ improves Micro-F1 (e.g., $0.6517 \to 0.6695$ for Mixup, $+1.8$ points). For unweighted Macro-F1, lowering $\tau$ to $0.25$ provides substantial recovery across all models ($+4.4$ points for \texttt{no\_aug} from $0.4910 \to 0.5346$; $+6.6$ points for Mixup from $0.4790 \to 0.5454$; and $+8.4$ points for Full Augmentation from $0.4347 \to 0.5185$). On validation data, operating at $\tau = 0.20$ achieves balanced operational recall ($67.6\%\text{--}69.9\%$) while retaining solid precision ($\approx 60.0\%\text{--}62.5\%$, specifically $60.66\%$ for \texttt{no\_aug}, $62.49\%$ for \texttt{mixup}, and $59.96\%$ for \texttt{full}), motivating its selection as our standardized operating threshold across held-out evaluations in Table~\ref{tab:setup1_architectures}.

\section{Discussion and Qualitative Error Analysis}

\subsection{Architectural Trade-offs and Inductive Biases}
Our empirical findings reveal complementary strengths across convolutional and Transformer paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Convolutional Efficiency:} Inverted residual networks (EfficientNet-B0 and EfficientNet-B4) demonstrate superior compute-to-accuracy efficiency on clean spectrograms, with EfficientNet-B4 achieving highest clean precision ($0.5396$ mAP, $0.6114$ F1) and EfficientNet-B0 providing extreme efficiency ($0.5233$ mAP at $1.05\text{ GMACs}$). Depthwise separable convolutions efficiently capture compact, local spectro-temporal textures. Under undefended noise, EfficientNet architectures exhibit the strongest resilience ($-51.0\%$ to $-51.6\%$ relative drop), whereas ResNet baselines degrade by $-54.5\%$ to $-56.1\%$; scaling from ViT-Tiny ($-59.7\%$) to ViT-Small ($-57.8\%$) closes the absolute margin relative to standard ResNets ($0.2231$ mAP at $-5\text{ dB}$).
    \item \textbf{Self-Attention and Global Receptive Fields:} Audio Spectrogram Transformers provide competitive discriminability ($0.5068\text{--}0.5288$ clean mAP), strong distribution ranking for ViT-Tiny ($0.9106$ mAUC), and high Top-1 retrieval for the scaled ViT-Small ($76.54\%$, exceeding all CNN baselines), hypothesized to benefit from global multi-head self-attention across distant frequency bands.
    \item \textbf{Token Pooling Flexibility:} ViT's patch tokenization allows dynamic learned attention pooling to selectively emphasize active acoustic events, outperforming rigid dual-token classification by $+0.013$ clean test mAP ($0.5068 \to 0.5199$) and providing an effective substrate for acoustic noise training.
\end{enumerate}

Because these degradation patterns are observed from the aggregate difference between \texttt{no\_aug} and \texttt{full}, the underlying failure mechanisms represent qualitative hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesized Mode 1: Erasure of High-Frequency and Fast Transients:} Sound events defined by short, rapid impulses (\texttt{Doorbell}, \texttt{Rattle}) or delicate high-frequency energy textures (\texttt{Packing\_tape}, \texttt{Frying\_(food)}) are particularly vulnerable under compound augmentation, likely when random spectral masks obscure their narrow discriminative spectro-temporal cues.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesized Mode 2: Polyphonic Mixture Confusion:} In classes characterized by subtle conversational speech (\texttt{Conversation}) or continuous low-amplitude engine rumbles (\texttt{Boat\_and\_vehicle}), linear convex combination with dominant background audio in Mixup can submerge the target event below the perceptual floor, degrading precision.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesized Mode 3: Disruption of Harmonic Resonance:} Melodic percussive instruments such as \texttt{Marimba} rely on precise harmonic relations between the fundamental strike and wooden resonator overtones; frequency masking distorts these relative overtone ratios.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
We highlight four methodological limitations of this study:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Compound Recipe in the Robust Model:} The proposed robust model combines four distinct elements: continuous colored noise, Schroeder reverberation, learned attention pooling, and class-balanced sampling (\texttt{DistributedWeightedSampler}), while omitting SpecAugment. Although head-matched comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:dsp_noise_gain} isolate a $+48.9\%\text{ to }+49.9\%$ relative gain ($+0.0997\text{ to }+0.1059$ mAP at $-5\text{ dB}$) largely attributable to physical acoustic synthesis, the recipe bundles these changes and the isolated impact of class-balanced sampling remains unablated.
    \item \textbf{Single-Seed Evaluations:} Due to compute budget constraints on FSD50K, experiments were conducted with a single random seed ($42$). While primary macro-level conclusions (e.g., the $+56.0\%$ relative noise gain, representing an absolute improvement of $+0.1142$ mAP at $-5\text{ dB}$) decisively exceed our adopted practical effect-size threshold ($\Delta \ge 0.010$), fine-grained margins below this threshold should be interpreted as empirical tendencies rather than definitive separations.
    \item \textbf{Architecture-Specific Learning Rate Tuning:} Learning rates were maintained at established AST and CMKD defaults ($5 \times 10^{-5}$ for Transformers, $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for CNNs) without exhaustive per-architecture grid searches, representing a practical constraint.
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Domain Scope:} The ACE evaluation benchmark comprises indoor room ambient noises (offices, lecture halls, HVAC fans, babble). While directly representative of indoor acoustic monitoring, outdoor soundscapes (turbulent open-air wind shear, high-speed traffic) involve complex acoustic dispersion patterns that warrant future outdoor benchmarking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Answers to Research Questions}
This investigation presented a systematic empirical benchmark of multi-label environmental sound recognition on FSD50K under clean and adverse acoustic conditions. Our findings provide direct answers to the four core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ1 (Architectural Inductive Bias):} Inverted residual convolutional networks (EfficientNet-B4 at $0.5396$ mAP, EfficientNet-B0 at $0.5233$ mAP) deliver the highest clean precision and parameter efficiency, while retaining the highest resilience under undefended noise ($-51.0\%$ relative drop at $-5\text{ dB}$). Audio Spectrogram Transformers deliver competitive discrimination ($0.5068\text{--}0.5288$ clean mAP), strong distribution ranking for ViT-Tiny ($0.9106$ mAUC), and superior Top-1 retrieval for the scaled ViT-Small ($76.54\%$, exceeding all CNN baselines), hypothesized to benefit from global multi-head self-attention across distant frequency patches.
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ2 (Token Pooling Mechanisms):} Attention pooling achieved the strongest held-out evaluation mAP ($0.5199$, $+0.0131$ over the dual-token baseline of $0.5068$), while unweighted GAP ($0.5126$, $\Delta = +0.0058$) and GAP+Max ($0.5051$) are not distinguishable from the baseline under our adopted practical effect-size threshold ($\Delta \ge 0.010$). Dynamic attention weighting over patch tokens provides a modest empirical advantage by focusing on localized sound bursts.
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ3 (Noise Robustness \& Acoustic Augmentation):} Undefended Transformers suffer severe collapse under out-of-distribution acoustic room noise (falling by $59.7\%$ relative mAP to $0.2040$ at $-5\text{ dB}$ SNR on the ACE benchmark). Our compound acoustic augmentation pipeline (continuous colored noise, Schroeder reverberation, Mixup, and attention pooling) establishes strong acoustic resilience, achieving $0.3182$ mAP at $-5\text{ dB}$ ($+56.0\%$ relative gain, $+0.1142$ absolute mAP over baseline ViT-Tiny, and $+49.9\%$ over the matched attention-pool model) and boosting clean evaluation to $0.5303$ mAP without external noise data.
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ4 (Augmentation Synergy \& Threshold Calibration):} On held-out clean evaluation, isolated spectral masking provides modest benefit ($+1.03$ points mAP for SpecAugment over baseline), while standalone Mixup delivers substantial regularization ($+2.55$ points mAP to $0.5124$, $+2.20$ points Top-1 hit to $75.97\%$). Operating under default uncalibrated decision thresholds ($\tau=0.50$) leads to severe tail-class recall compression (with Full Augmentation exhibiting the lowest Macro-F1 at $0.3974$ vs. $0.4378$ for baseline), which is effectively resolved by lowering decision thresholds to $\tau \in [0.20, 0.25]$ (recovering Macro-F1 by $+4.4$ to $+8.4$ points across models).
\end{itemize}
